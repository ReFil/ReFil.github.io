Powering the Gigaset S810H from USB Type C

DATE

Headers

One thing i didnt have during my time at 39c3 last year was a DECT phone, and whilst there was the GSM network (for which eSIMs ran out almost immediately) and the SiP network it wasn't very reliable, so for EMF I got some cheap DECT handsets on eBay. Their internal charging dock is a bit of a pain to haul around, so I was thinking it'd be a lot nicer if you could just charge one with USB C, and whilst I'm sure I'm not the first one to do this I hadn't seen any previous attempts at it online, so endeavoured to find out how hard it was (spoiler, not very!)

The Gigaset S810H and its charging dock

In it's day I think the S810H was a rather fancy model from Gigaset, but TBH i chose it because it had the widest compatibility according to the eventphone compatibility list but wasn't obscenely expensive like the Mitel models. For £35 for two and two charging docks you can't complain. Like almost all cordless phones the S810H uses a pair of NiMH AAA batteries for power, and they get recharged when you put it on the dock. The first thing i did was take a multimeter to the dock and discover it puts out whatever the hell voltage it likes, I dont know if that's a specific issue with my dock but what the power adapter said was 4V out I measured at over 8V once, and 7V another time. Phone doesn't seem to mind though. Next I took the basic charging dock apart (I got one charging only dock and one dock phone line dect base station doodad) and discovered it was literally just two metal contacts, not even a PCB, so that means the charging brains are in the phone.

Next step was pop the phone apart and try and figure out how it works, there's not much in them. They have one big monolithic chip that does everything, a memory chip for it and that's most of it. Googling the part number (Insert part number here) wasn'mt very revealing in terms of datasheets but I hunted my way down to a brochure for this family which mentioned integrated charge control capabilities. So there's no external IC that needs hacking or modifying. Given the voltage the base was outputting I decided to just send it and put 5V from my variable power supply to the charger terminals on the bottom of the phone ands it went bloop and started very happily charging up.

Fitting a Type C port in it

Now I knew it just needed DC at the right spot to charge I could get to the more fun part of actually fitting it in, there's not a lot of space in the phone. Originally I tried hacking up some random type c TP4054 board I had floating around but it just wouldn't fit. The solution ended up being USB C to micro USB adapter boards, as the connector shell is trimmed back relative to a normal type C one, which I believe is permissible under the type C mechanical standards. They also have the pcb with the 5.1k resistors (mandatory for any type C device, i've lost count of the number of even reputable products that just dont bother with them) mounted flat to the back of the connector rather than perpendicular to it. The trimmed shell doesn't support the cable as much so I wouldn't spin the phone above my head by the cable but if you're careful it's fine. I melted the hole into the bottom of the frame with a soldering iron, tacked it in place with some superglue then followed up with some hot glue to really lock it down. I'm a fan of UV curing resin but the aliexpres LOCTTLF (I'm not joking that's actually the brand) glue I have remains elastic and likes to creep over time so this is not the ideal use case for it. Couple wires from the board to the metal case and trimming all the plastic in the way and it went together a treat

After confirming everything worked I gave it the worst coat of paint I've ever given anything in my life, i'm genuinely ashamed of what I did to it, and just used it for EMF (and then at work because it's more reliable than the panasonic dects at work). Apart from the paint coming off and making me and everyone else who held it blue it was great, gave no issues at all. All I'm going to do before 40c3 is give it a coat of black paint that might last better. It even still charges on the original base if you don't have a Type C available
