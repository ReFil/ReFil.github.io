Driving the ReMarkable 1 Display: Part 1

27th November 2024

When I was doing my undergraduate degree, I had a ReMarkable 1. It was great for taking notes and doodling ideas and i found it useful in the first year of my PhD too... Until it stopped working. It was a fairly gradual failure, it popped up error messages about not saving or accessing the flash occasionally, then very often then it just stopped booting. My guess at the time was the EMMC chip that ran the show had given out to wear or something, but there's recovery utilities online and I fancy my skills with the soldering iron so I ordered a couple replacement chips from aliexpress, soldered one in place with hot air and tried reimaging the chip.

It wasn't very successful, I couldn't get my linux machine to image the flash properly. I never figured out what the issue was but something about the process or my soldering wasn't right. I couldn't bring myself to throw the guts of it away, so I stashed them away in a drawer. Recently I've had the thought that it would make a very nice display, not sure what for but I've driven dinky little E-Inks on keyboards, so how hard can it be? I took a look at the published kernel devicetree and u-boot source but I know nothing about this stuff, both reference the screen being 334 pixels tall when it's 1872 and don't have any clues towards what driver IC they used.

This is because they don't have a discrete driver IC on the display. I found a listing for the screen on AliExpress (god bless aliexpress) with a part number (ES103TD1) and it turns out a display with a slightly different part number has an open datasheet, the ES103TC1. The display has a whole bunch of source and gate pins, a dead giveaway for a display expecting an external controller, but when you look on the board you can't see a discrete controller. There's a PMIC, a couple of RAM chips and the main iMX6 CPU and EMMC and a WiFi controller under a can. There's another PMIC at the other end of the motherboard and that's it. Turns out some variants of the iMX6 have an integrated EPD controller which is very neat, but it makes my life harder.
