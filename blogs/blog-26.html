I'm so tired of the slop era

DATE

This is not exactly my usual sort of project blog post but I don't use mainstream social media and have wanted to write down my thoughts on this for a while. LLMs, "AI", slop machines, codex, Claude, ChatGPT or whatever the latest one is this week (last i heard there was something called Jev??) are the latest (and I use this term extremely charitably) "innovation" the big tech world has delivered unto us peons, alongside this months barrage of catastrophic data breaches, zero days and Windows updates that manage to continually decrease the functionality of my work laptop. Discussion on its use is polarised, with a large group of people fundamentally opposed to their usage, and another group completely enamoured with the technology, it's current uses and it's potential.

I do not like this technology, the way it is marketed nor the way it is used. The benefits cannot outweight the massive harms caused to the world by these systems. My interaction with these systems is primarilyt through a technical lens, after all I work in a technical field, but it's not possible to coldly view these systems as code completion engines, intellisense but for ideas. The same LLM that's ruining your codebase is sending someone else into a psychosis episode that will end with them ending their life. Engineering and programming are creative fields, and despite the god complex that many engineers have they are not immune to the sycophancy machine

Part of me wonders why bother listing the pros and cons of generative AI usage like it's going to change anyone's mind, but I feel like it should be done regardless for completeness' sake if nothing else and to display the mass disparity beteen the benefits and disadvantages (some of which are insurmountable) of this technology.

Why I care and why you should care

Generative AI tools have made being on the internet a less enjoyable and pleasant experience. Ignoring the technofacist horrors they're enabling on a massive scale they have ruined the fun I used to have looking through Hackaday articles and browing new developments on GitHub and suchlike. Nowadays it's a crapshoot whether the project will be some delicately crafted masterpiece clearly born from the creators dedication and passion for the art of programming or is it unnecessarily long and unnecessarily complex. There are some isntances whhen I can look past egregiously vibe coded actual code, but when the associated project documentation, README, website and even blogpost are all clearly generated then whats actually the achievement here. If the creator didn't even have the passion in what "they" made to write it up in their own words then why should I assume they had the passion to check it actually works properly. I've seen many instances of vibe projects that have a very impressve suite of CI tests and yet they still miss egregious bugs that were obvious to anyone actually using the project, so all the big CI suite achieved was wasting massive amounts of GitHub actions time and instilling a false sense of overconfidence. I also fear for the deskilling of a generation of engineers and programmers. Having previously done teaching the influence of LLMs on study and learning has been pretty catastrophics. Students were not learning techniques to improve their knowledge of what they were doing, they were treating the whole thing as a box ticking exercise.

The labour theft involved with generative AI usage doesn't end with the training process either. Generative tools are excellent at creating large bodies of work that look convincing at first glance, require lots of time to review, at which point they completely fall apart, usually requiring the reviewer to redo the job from scratch having only wasted more time.

They have also made some of my work more frustrating and less fun. As a maintainer of public facing open source projects it is inevitable that LLM generated code, issues and PRs would rock up and whilst sometimes the work makes sense and the submitter is respectful of the knowledge gap between the maintainer and someone prompting Claude other times they are not and will generate an unreasonably large code change based on some very vauge prompt like "Fix this problem", get the LLM to summarise these changes in an unnecessarily verbose and patronising manner, dump that pulled hand grenade in my lap and expect me to untangle the mess. To put it bluntly I do not care about the technical merits of some subset of a large patch when it is delivered to me without respect and understanding for the time and knowledge it takes to review a PR in a more in depth manner than "LGTM :thumbsup:".

This is an experience shared by many technical projects. decomp.dev have recently made the decision to stop accepting new decompilation projects because the whole field and community is starting to be overrun by slop. Reverse engineering and decompilation is a field where LLMs can provide useful assistance when used carefully, but the zone has been flooded by people not passionate for the process of decompiling an old retro classic but merely looking for another achievement to add to their tally, another repo to try and get that hockey stick stars chart on GitHub. Archaeologics found ancient bronze age ruins in a mineshaft, were delicately extracting them and slopsmiths said "we can do that faster" and broke out the C4 like that was ever the intention of the exercsise.
