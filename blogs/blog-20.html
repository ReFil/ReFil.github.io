Twing Raid 2026, A retrospective

DATE

I got back from the Twing Raid 2026, and it was an absolutely incredible experience I will remember forever. This is the experience my best friend and I had doing it, although if you are to embark on this journey yourself your experience will vary depending on your approach to the event, your preparation, and to a lesser extent your french skills! Over time I'm also going to write up how we prepared the car and ourselves for the rally, as that's worth documenting, especially if you're not from France/dont speak fluent french. When we first registered for the event a lot of the documentation was exclusively in French, it did change as more international teams joined but it's still a primarily french event, and you will benefit enormously from being able to speak even just basic taxi French. All days are with respect to the official day scheduling for the event, given we had a long journey to do just to get to FICOBA in Irun, Spain, we set off several days before the start of the event

Day -2: Final Prep

As with all of these sorts of projects you never have enough time, so the day before we departed we had some minor jobs to do. The alternator belt was making a right racket so we tensioned that up, which was complicated by yours truly absolutely mullering a riv nut in the belly pan we installed. We also packed the car with everything we could in advance to save us doing it at the crack of dawn the next day, as well as making a trip to Sainsburys to get the food for the solidarity donation. This was also the day I woke up to a text from Brittany Ferries saying our original UK to France travel plan (The Portsmouth to Ouistreham ferry) had been cancelled, so we scrambled to book the channel tunnel LeShuttle service instead, which was a bit more expensive but really no big deal in the grand sceme of things. Final things was give the car a good clean and finish stickering it up, we planned to not clean it until we got back to build up a nice patina.

Day -1: Hampshire to Le Mans

We made a reasonably early start on this day, which would be a recurring theme for the next two weeks. The trip inside the UK was very uneventful, the M25 and M20 presented no fuss at all and we got to the LeShuttle terminal with loads of time to spare, this let us get an earlier train than anticipated which is always nice. The first few days I hadn't mastered the correct posture for driving the car and sitting in the drivers seat for longer than an hour or two absolutely killed my back, but I got a good posture figured out eventually. Once on the french AutoRoutes we discovered a fun problem, the speed sensor went on the fritz and would drop the speed to 0 KPH, which would also just turn off the power steering, this is mildly inconvenient but not the end of the world, we originally planned to try and get a new one in Le Mans at the French equivalent of a Euro Car Parts, but ended up going a different route. With the big knobbly tyres and relatively sedate performance overtaking was always a hilarious affair, but given enough time we could get up to 130kph and hold it there and just fly across France. The fuel economy we were measuring was absolutely shocking, but we did end up figuring out that was partially due to the speed sensor, as it wouldn't increment the odometer or trip counter when it wasn't working. We figured we were getting 350km to a ~35L tank which was surprisingly bad. We stayed the night in a cheap Airbnb in Le Mans, which was a bit sketchy to get in but was reasonably nice. This was also about the time I made my yearly re discovery of the fact that continental europe really isn't it for vegans, so my dietary choices were rather limited throughout the duration of this trip.

Day 0: Le Mans to Irun

We originally intended to start the day by heading to the Le Mans 24H museum, and taking a picture with the twingo in front of the circuit's big overhead sign, but unfortunately it was just our luck the museum was shut for renovations with lorries in the front area so there was no good photo opportunity. At least we could drive down the mulsanne straight and give it some beans around some of the corners. The speed sensor was getting progressively more flaky, so we found one on spanish Amazon and had it delivered to an Amazon locker close to the rest stop on the first day, as well as a few new bolts for the back plate if we needed to fight the riv nuts off to do the job. For some strange reason the car felt meaningfully quicker today, it was absolutely flying along. We were happily cruising at 120-130Kph no issues, and saw on the facebook group for the event other teams reporting maxing out at 100. We think this is a combination of us having one of the two variants of higher power engine, and not weighing ourselves down with roof racks full of crap. Towards the late afternoon as we really homed in on Irun we saw some other raiders, in alpine and espace F1 themed twingos which looked really great. We stuck around with them for a while but ended up leaving them in the dust because they were just too slow. We finished the evening in another nice Airbnb, a little guest house run by some retired hoteliers. We grabbed some food in town, repacked the car to prepare for the next day and set alarms for a 7AM start. Each team is given a specific time to turn up for the mandatory checks, because we booked early we were able to go on the morning of departure, half the teams needed to be there the evening before.
